# Design

## Cause, with sources

Android 14's JobScheduler, at commit 6e47c7075b91983ae501114425ea25e6df7690c8 of platform/frameworks/base (the android14-release branch head):

- `JobServiceContext.onServiceDisconnected` closes the job with "unexpectedly disconnected" and `needsReschedule = true`, with no stop reason set, so the stop counts as a system stop: https://android.googlesource.com/platform/frameworks/base/+/6e47c7075b91983ae501114425ea25e6df7690c8/apex/jobscheduler/service/java/com/android/server/job/JobServiceContext.java (lines 990 to 1006).
- `JobSchedulerService.getRescheduleJobForFailureLocked` counts it in `numSystemStops`, and computes `backoffAttempts = numFailures + numSystemStops / SYSTEM_STOP_TO_FAILURE_RATIO`, with a default ratio of 3. For the first two system stops that is 0, and then `earliestRuntimeMs = JobStatus.NO_EARLIEST_RUNTIME`: the new job has no timing delay (same file's sibling, JobSchedulerService.java, lines 2781 to 2869).
- `onJobCompletedLocked` tracks the rescheduled job with `startTrackingJobLocked` and posts no MSG_CHECK_JOB (lines 3023 to 3091). A job that schedule() adds is queued at once when ready (lines 1804 to 1817), but a rescheduled one isn't. The job waits, ready and not pending, until a controller reports a change or something posts a full check, such as the screen turning on or off, or a change of the app's process state. That is what #269's dumpsys shows: `Ready: true (... !pending=true !active=true ...)`.
- "started as UIJ: false" only means that this JobStatus never started: JobServiceContext sets `startedAsUserInitiatedJob` when it starts a job (line 547), and JobStatus prints it in its dump (JobStatus.java line 2815). The job is still treated as user-initiated (`userInitiatedApproved: true`).
- Android 13's code (android13-release) computes `backoffAttempts = getNumFailures() + 1`, so every reschedule has a delay, and TimeController's alarm reports the job when the delay ends. That is why WorkManager's job on Android 10 to 13 went on 20 to 45 seconds after the kill, with WorkManager's 30-second backoff.
- The same code, `backoffAttempts == 0` and no check after a reschedule, is in android15-release, android16-release and main (main at 1cdfff555f4a21f71ccc978290e2e212e2f8b168). Android 17's emulator started the job again at once in #54's checks, so some other event there makes JobScheduler look at its jobs. I haven't found which, so the fix doesn't rely on it.
- The app can't schedule the UIDT job again from the background: Android 14's `ActivityManagerService.canScheduleUserInitiatedJobs` allows it only while the app is visible or has a few other privileges, none of which a background job has (same commit, services/core/java/com/android/server/am/ActivityManagerService.java, lines 6227 to 6337). The UIDT page says the same: they must be scheduled "while the application is visible to the user" (https://developer.android.com/develop/background-work/background-tasks/uidt).

So nothing in Dewfall's job is wrong, and no setting of the UIDT job changes it: UIDT jobs can't have a minimum latency, and the backoff criteria apply only from the third system stop.

## Fix

A backup job that JobScheduler starts after a timing delay, which is the path that works on Android 13.

1. `:core:download` gets two plain JobScheduler job IDs, 55 and 56, for DownloadJobService, beside the UIDT job's 54. Both are below WorkManager's range of 1,000 to 100,000. A backup job is NETWORK_TYPE_ANY, not persisted (the persisted UIDT job covers a reboot), with a minimum latency of 30 seconds, the same as BACKOFF_SECONDS, the same backoff, and PRIORITY_HIGH (API 33 and later). On Android 14, FlexibilityController is off by default, `DEFAULT_FLEXIBILITY_ENABLED = false`, with one 72-hour fallback for every priority (https://android.googlesource.com/platform/frameworks/base/+/6e47c7075b91983ae501114425ea25e6df7690c8/apex/jobscheduler/service/java/com/android/server/job/controllers/FlexibilityController.java, lines 685 and 689). On AOSP main, `DEFAULT_APPLIED_CONSTRAINTS = 0` keeps it off by default too, and its fallbacks depend on priority: 6 hours for PRIORITY_HIGH and 12 hours for PRIORITY_DEFAULT (https://android.googlesource.com/platform/frameworks/base/+/1cdfff555f4a21f71ccc978290e2e212e2f8b168/apex/jobscheduler/service/java/com/android/server/job/controllers/FlexibilityController.java, lines 1175 and 1191 to 1195). So where a device's server config turns flexibility on, a backup job may wait for charging or idle for hours, less with HIGH from that code on. ADR 0010's note says this plainly.
2. While the UIDT job runs, DownloadJobService keeps backup job 55 armed 30 seconds ahead: it schedules it at the start of the run and again every 10 seconds, which replaces the pending one, so while the run is alive the backup never starts. When the run ends normally, with DONE or RETRY, the service cancels the backup, after its keeper has stopped, so no late tick arms it again. The service turns a run that throws into RETRY inside the keeper's block, so a throw also ends with the backup cancelled and jobFinished's reschedule, which has a backoff delay. The keeper itself cancels its backup on a return or a throw, and leaves it armed only when it is cancelled. The service cancels the backup because jobFinished's own reschedule has a backoff delay and goes on by itself. When Android stops the job (onStopJob) for any reason but the user's, or the process ends, the last backup stays armed and starts within about 30 seconds. When onStopJob's reason is STOP_REASON_USER (`isStopByUser`), the run has paused the queue, so the service cancels the backup too. When a backup job's reason is STOP_REASON_CANCELLED_BY_APP, the UIDT job replaced or cancelled it, so once its keeper has stopped it cancels the backup it kept armed, so no late tick leaves a stray backup, but only when no run with that backup's ID is registered in the service: a registered run with that ID, a UIDT run's own 55 or another backup run, owns it. At its start the UIDT job also cancels backup 56.
3. When a backup job starts and no UIDT job runs in the service, it runs the same DownloadRunner as a plain job, with the notification of step 7, and keeps the other backup ID armed the same way, so a process that ends during a backup run goes on too. A backup run that ends with RETRY asks to run again, with its backoff. If any run is registered in the service, a UIDT run or another backup run, the backup does nothing and finishes, returning false from onStartJob. That matters after a kill during a backup run on 55: JobScheduler reschedules 55 with no delay, the same way as #269, so 55 sits ready, and when 56 starts a new process 30 seconds later, the ready 55 may start in the same process at about the same time. Whichever starts second does nothing, so the two never stop each other, and the one that runs keeps the other armed as its backup.
4. A backup run that ends DONE cancels UIDT job 54, which the kill left ready but idle, so it doesn't start later at a screen change, find nothing, and show its notification and a Task Manager entry for a moment. Cancelling a job is allowed from the background. It cancels only while the run phase is IDLE, holding the run state's mutex, so it never cancels a job that a tap scheduled after the run's last claim, because QueueScheduler.startNow sets SCHEDULED under the same mutex.
5. When the UIDT job starts again, for example because Android looked at its jobs again, its first schedule of backup 55 replaces a running backup 55, which JobScheduler stops, and its cancel of 56 stops a running 56. The stopped backup run puts its download back to waiting with its bytes, as any stop does, and the runner's run lock makes the UIDT run wait for that. So the UIDT job takes the download back with its notification.
6. When the app shows, or the user taps a download control, while a backup run downloads, QueueScheduler schedules the UIDT job, which replaces the waiting rescheduled one and takes the download back as in step 5. DownloadRunState gets a flag for a running backup run, which the service sets and clears, and which a UIDT start clears and a successful launch clears, so the scheduler hands back once per backup run, and tries again at the next start if Android refused that launch. Today the scheduler does nothing while a run is active, and it still doesn't reset rows while one is.
7. A backup run's notification. Any running job may give Android a notification with `JobService.setNotification`, not only a UIDT job: on Android 14, JobServiceContext.doSetNotification checks only the caller's UID (https://android.googlesource.com/platform/frameworks/base/+/6e47c7075b91983ae501114425ea25e6df7690c8/apex/jobscheduler/service/java/com/android/server/job/JobServiceContext.java, lines 934 to 962), and JobNotificationCoordinator.enqueueNotification posts the notification for any running job, adding FLAG_USER_INITIATED_JOB only for a job that started as a UIDT job (https://android.googlesource.com/platform/frameworks/base/+/6e47c7075b91983ae501114425ea25e6df7690c8/apex/jobscheduler/service/java/com/android/server/job/JobNotificationCoordinator.java, lines 113 to 180). When the job ends, including when its process dies, which closes the job through closeAndCleanupJobLocked, removeNotificationAssociation cancels it under JOB_END_NOTIFICATION_POLICY_REMOVE, unless another running job still uses the same ID (same file, lines 182 to 220). So a backup run calls `setNotification` as the UIDT job does, but first only once it has claimed a download, at its first progress report, because a plain job, unlike a UIDT job, needn't give a notification within 10 seconds, and a backup run that finds nothing, such as after a Stop from the Task Manager, must show nothing: the same "Downloading a video" notification, the same ID, 2001, and REMOVE. Android then takes it away when the run ends or its process dies, so nothing is left over after a kill. When the UIDT job takes the download back, it sets 2001 first and only then arms 55, which stops the backup; the backup's end then leaves 2001 up, because the UIDT job uses it. Android doesn't list a backup job in its Task Manager, because the flag and the Task Manager entry are only for jobs that started as UIDT jobs. The notification isn't marked as a UIDT job's, so on Android 14 and later the user can swipe it away, and each later update would post it again. So before each update the backup run checks `NotificationManager.getActiveNotifications`: once it has seen 2001 there and it is gone again, the user swiped it away, and the run calls setNotification no more. It counts a swipe only after it has seen the notification once, because posting is asynchronous and NotificationManagerService rate-limits an app's updates, and without the notification permission it is never seen, so it is never counted as swiped. A hand check on Android 14 and 17 confirms that the notification shows during a backup run, goes when the run ends and when the process dies, and that the Task Manager doesn't list the app. On Android 17, where the UIDT job starts again at once after a kill, the check forces a backup run with `adb shell cmd jobscheduler cancel -u 0 io.github.dewfallapp 54` during a UIDT run: the run stops with a reason that isn't the user's, so backup 55 stays armed and starts 30 seconds later.
8. A Stop from the Task Manager ends the process without telling the job, and Android doesn't reschedule the UIDT job ("Prevents user-visible jobs from being rescheduled", https://developer.android.com/develop/background-work/background-tasks/uidt). Backup 55 is an ordinary job, so it stays armed and starts a new process about 30 seconds later. The download stays paused because of DownloadsAfterStop, which already runs at every process start before anything reads the queue: DewfallApplication's onCreate starts its check, and the runner awaits it before its reset. It finds the exit record's REASON_USER_REQUESTED with a running row and pauses the queue, so the backup run finds nothing to claim, ends DONE, posts no notification, and disarms. The fix relies on this, and the hand check confirms it. Force stop cancels the app's jobs, so no backup runs after it.
9. A swipe from the recent apps during a backup run. A backup run's process is in the background: a regular job binds with `BIND_NOT_FOREGROUND | BIND_NOT_PERCEPTIBLE`, unlike a UIDT job (JobServiceContext.java at 6e47c707, lines 419 to 443). On a swipe, ActivityManagerService's `killProcessesForRemovedTask` kills such a process at once with `REASON_USER_REQUESTED` and `SUBREASON_REMOVE_TASK`, and only sets a process that isn't in the background to be killed later (ActivityManagerService.java at 6e47c707, lines 17857 to 17875; ActivityTaskSupervisor.killTaskProcessesIfPossible). The next process start would then read REASON_USER_REQUESTED with a DOWNLOADING row, and DownloadsAfterStop would pause the queue, which the spec forbids. To tell the swipe apart:
   - A backup run writes a marker file, `download-backup-run` in noBackupFilesDir, and a UIDT run deletes it. The file is empty: it says only that a backup run ran last, and holds nothing about a video (ADR 0007). Both do it inside the runner's run, after `stopCheck.await()` and while holding `runState.runLock`, before the first claim: DownloadRunner.run gets a `begin` callback that runOnDispatcher calls at that point. So DownloadsAfterStop's decision, which runs once per process and which the runner awaits, always reads the marker that the previous process left, never one that this process's jobs wrote or deleted. That matters both ways: after a Stop during a UIDT run, backup 55 starts a new process, and its marker must not hide the Stop; after a swipe during a backup run, a UIDT job that starts first in the new process must not delete the marker before the decision reads it.
   - At the process's start, DownloadsAfterStop doesn't pause when the marker is there and a backup job is still scheduled: `JobScheduler.getPendingJob` for 55 or 56 isn't null. It reads JobScheduler's list of the app's jobs, which holds running jobs too (JobSchedulerService.getPendingJob at 6e47c707, lines 1991 to 2002).
   - Why that can only be a swipe: the Task Manager never lists a backup run, so a REASON_USER_REQUESTED exit while the marker is there is a swipe or Force stop, and Force stop cancels every job of the app ("app force stopped", JobSchedulerService.java at 6e47c707, lines 1415 to 1430), so after it no backup is scheduled and the queue pauses as before. A Stop from the Task Manager during a UIDT run finds no marker, so it pauses as before. On Android 10 to 13 no backup runs, so nothing changes there.
   - `pausesAfterExit` gets the two facts as parameters, with JVM tests for each case: a swipe during a backup run with a backup scheduled doesn't pause; Force stop during a backup run (marker, no backup) pauses; a Stop during a UIDT run (no marker, backup scheduled) pauses; and the existing cases stay.
10. Android 10 to 13 keep the WorkManager path unchanged.

Limits of a backup run, from the same commit: a regular job may run for up to 30 minutes (`RUNTIME_FREE_QUOTA_MAX_LIMIT_MS`) while the phone charges or the app is in the foreground, and otherwise until its standby bucket's quota runs out (QuotaController at 6e47c707, lines 3111 to 3132: about 10 minutes of run time in each window, where the window is 10 minutes for ACTIVE, 2 hours for WORKING_SET, 8 hours for FREQUENT and 24 hours for RARE), with 10 minutes guaranteed (`RUNTIME_MIN_GUARANTEE_MS`). So a big file's backup run may stop partway through. The next run goes on from the file's length after the job's backoff, or once quota returns, losing at most one 10 MiB range, as ADR 0010 already says of expedited work on Android 10 to 13.

The effect that users see: during a backup run, the usual notification shows when the app's notifications are allowed, Android's Task Manager doesn't list the backup job, and the notification goes when the run ends or its process dies, as the maintainer chose (proposal.md, answers 2 and 3).

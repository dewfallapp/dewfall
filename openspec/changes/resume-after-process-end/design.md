# Design

## Cause, with sources

Android 14's JobScheduler, at commit 6e47c7075b91983ae501114425ea25e6df7690c8 of platform/frameworks/base (the android14-release branch head):

- `JobServiceContext.onServiceDisconnected` closes the job with "unexpectedly disconnected" and `needsReschedule = true`, with no stop reason set, so the stop counts as a system stop: https://android.googlesource.com/platform/frameworks/base/+/6e47c7075b91983ae501114425ea25e6df7690c8/apex/jobscheduler/service/java/com/android/server/job/JobServiceContext.java (lines 990 to 1006).
- `JobSchedulerService.getRescheduleJobForFailureLocked` counts it in `numSystemStops`, and computes `backoffAttempts = numFailures + numSystemStops / SYSTEM_STOP_TO_FAILURE_RATIO`, with a default ratio of 3. For the first two system stops that is 0, and then `earliestRuntimeMs = JobStatus.NO_EARLIEST_RUNTIME`: the new job has no timing delay (same file's sibling, JobSchedulerService.java, lines 2781 to 2869).
- `onJobCompletedLocked` tracks the rescheduled job with `startTrackingJobLocked` and posts no MSG_CHECK_JOB (lines 3023 to 3091). A job that schedule() adds is queued at once when ready (lines 1804 to 1817), but a rescheduled one isn't. The job waits, ready and not pending, until a controller reports a change or something posts a full check, such as the screen turning on or off, or a change of the app's process state. That is what #269's dumpsys shows: `Ready: true (... !pending=true !active=true ...)`.
- "started as UIJ: false" only means that this JobStatus never started: JobServiceContext sets `startedAsUserInitiatedJob` when it starts a job (line 547), and JobStatus prints it in its dump (JobStatus.java line 2815). The job is still treated as user-initiated (`userInitiatedApproved: true`).
- Android 13's code (android13-release) computes `backoffAttempts = getNumFailures() + 1`, so every reschedule has a delay, and TimeController's alarm reports the job when the delay ends. That is why WorkManager's job on Android 10 to 13 went on 20 to 45 seconds after the kill, with WorkManager's 30-second backoff.
- The same code, `backoffAttempts == 0` and no check after a reschedule, is in android15-release, android16-release and main (main at 1cdfff555f4a21f71ccc978290e2e212e2f8b168). Android 17's emulator started the job again at once in #54's checks, so some other event there makes JobScheduler look at its jobs. I haven't found which, so the fix doesn't rely on it.
- The app can't schedule the UIDT job again from the background: Android 14's `ActivityManagerService.canScheduleUserInitiatedJobs` allows it only while the app is visible or has a few other privileges, none of which a background job has (same commit, services/core/java/com/android/server/am/ActivityManagerService.java, lines 6227 to 6337). The UIDT page says the same: they must be scheduled "while the application is visible to the user" (https://developer.android.com/develop/background-work/background-tasks/uidt).

So nothing in Dewfall's job is wrong, and no setting of the UIDT job changes it: UIDT jobs can't have a minimum latency, and the backoff criteria apply only from the third system stop.

## Fix

A backup job that JobScheduler starts after a timing delay, which is the path that works on Android 13.

1. `:core:download` gets two plain JobScheduler job IDs, 55 and 56, for DownloadJobService, beside the UIDT job's 54. Both are under WorkManager's range of 1,000 to 100,000. A backup job is NETWORK_TYPE_ANY, not persisted (the persisted UIDT job covers a reboot), with a minimum latency of 30 seconds, the same as BACKOFF_SECONDS, and the same backoff.
2. While the UIDT job runs, DownloadJobService keeps backup job 55 armed 30 seconds ahead: it schedules it at the start of the run and again every 10 seconds, which replaces the pending one, so while the run is alive the backup never starts. When the run ends normally, with DONE or RETRY, the service cancels the backup, because jobFinished's own reschedule has a backoff delay and goes on by itself. When Android stops the job (onStopJob) or the process ends, the last backup stays armed and starts within about 30 seconds. At its start the UIDT job also cancels backup 56.
3. When a backup job starts and no UIDT job runs in the service, it runs the same DownloadRunner as a plain job, with the notification of step 6, and keeps the other backup ID armed the same way, so a process that ends during a backup run goes on too. A backup run that ends with RETRY asks to run again, with its backoff. If a UIDT job runs in the service, the backup does nothing and finishes.
4. When the UIDT job starts again, for example because Android looked at its jobs again, its first schedule of backup 55 replaces a running backup 55, which JobScheduler stops, and its cancel of 56 stops a running 56. The stopped backup run puts its download back to waiting with its bytes, as any stop does, and the runner's run lock makes the UIDT run wait for that. So the UIDT job takes the download back with its notification.
5. When the app shows, or the user taps a download control, while a backup run downloads, QueueScheduler schedules the UIDT job, which replaces the waiting rescheduled one and takes the download back as in step 4. DownloadRunState gets a flag for a running backup run, which the service sets and clears, and which a UIDT start clears, so the scheduler hands back once per backup run. Today the scheduler does nothing while a run is active, and it still doesn't reset rows while one is.
6. A backup run's notification. Android gives a plain job no notification, so DownloadNotifications posts the same "Downloading a video" notification itself with NotificationManager, on the "Downloads" channel, under its own ID, 2002, so that taking it away never takes away the UIDT job's 2001. It posts nothing when the app's notifications aren't allowed (`NotificationManager.areNotificationsEnabled`). It updates on each percent change, as the UIDT job does. Before each update it checks `NotificationManager.getActiveNotifications`: once a posted notification is gone, the user swiped it away, and the run posts it no more. The run takes it away when it ends, whatever the outcome, and the UIDT job takes it away at its start, which covers one that a process that ended during a backup run left behind. A kill during a backup run can leave it up until the next run replaces it or takes it away, about 30 seconds later.
7. Android 10 to 13 keep the WorkManager path unchanged.

Limits of a backup run, from the same commit: a regular job may run for up to 30 minutes (`RUNTIME_FREE_QUOTA_MAX_LIMIT_MS`) while the phone charges or the app is in the foreground, and otherwise until its standby bucket's quota runs out (QuotaController: 10 minutes per window, 2 hours for WORKING_SET, 8 hours for FREQUENT), with 10 minutes guaranteed (`RUNTIME_MIN_GUARANTEE_MS`). So a big file's backup run may stop partway through. The next run goes on from the file's length after the job's backoff, or once quota returns, losing at most one 10 MiB range, as ADR 0010 already says of expedited work on Android 10 to 13.

The effect that users see: during a backup run, the usual notification shows when the app's notifications are allowed, Android's Task Manager doesn't list the app, and the notification can stay up for about 30 seconds after a process that ended during a backup run, as the maintainer chose (proposal.md, answers 1 and 2).

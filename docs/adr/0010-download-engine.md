# 0010. Download engine

## Status

Accepted

## Context

Phase 6 lets the user download a video and watch it without internet, and Phase 7 downloads new videos overnight with the same code ([roadmap](../roadmap.md)). AGENTS.md says download logic must not depend on any screen being open. With NewPipeExtractor v0.27.1, a video's sound and picture are separate files that HTTP range requests read, their links expire after some hours, and every request for their bytes must go through the YouTube layer's MediaRequests, with playback's rules ([architecture: Network access](../architecture.md#network-access)). A user-started download and an overnight one have different needs: the first starts at once on any connection, as the maintainer chose on November 26, 2026, and the second waits for Wi-Fi and charging. The facts below were checked on November 26, 2026.

Three engines were compared:

- **Media3's DownloadManager and DownloadService.** On API 31 and above, DownloadService never uses its Scheduler: `canStartForegroundServiceFromBackground` is `SDK_INT < 31`, and `getScheduler`'s Javadoc says the method "will not be called" there. Instead the service "will remain in the foreground until the downloads are able to continue" ([DownloadService.java at 1.11.1](https://github.com/androidx/media/blob/1.11.1/libraries/exoplayer/src/main/java/androidx/media3/exoplayer/offline/DownloadService.java)). An overnight wait for charging would hold a dataSync foreground service, which Android 15 and later allow "a total of 6 hours in a 24-hour period" ([Foreground service timeouts](https://developer.android.com/develop/background-work/services/fgs/timeout)). A DownloadRequest holds one URI, so a video would be two downloads, and the bytes live as SimpleCache spans that the guide says not to read directly ([Downloading media](https://developer.android.com/media/media3/exoplayer/downloading-media)).
- **WorkManager only.** Its guide says: "If you need to download data in response to a user action, consider using a user-initiated data transfer job" ([Support for long-running workers](https://developer.android.com/develop/background-work/background-tasks/persistent/how-to/long-running)). From Android 16, jobs started while the app is visible, and jobs beside a foreground service, "adhere to the job runtime quota", which "impacts tasks scheduled using WorkManager, JobScheduler, and DownloadManager" ([Android 16 behavior changes](https://developer.android.com/about/versions/16/behavior-changes-all)).
- **User-initiated data transfer (UIDT) jobs.** Google recommends them for transfers "triggered by the user" and WorkManager for those started in the background ([Data transfer options](https://developer.android.com/develop/background-work/background-tasks/data-transfer-options)). They exist from Android 14, need RUN_USER_INITIATED_JOBS, must be scheduled "while the application is visible to the user", and are "unaffected by App Standby Buckets quotas". "There is currently no Jetpack library that supports UIDT jobs", and the page names "WorkManager's foreground service implementation as a fallback" ([User-initiated data transfer](https://developer.android.com/develop/background-work/background-tasks/uidt)).

## Decision

- Dewfall writes the files itself. FormatWriter in `:core:download` asks for each format in 10 MiB byte ranges, the size yt-dlp uses for YouTube's HTTPS formats (`CHUNK_SIZE = 10 << 20` in [_video.py at 2026.11.23](https://github.com/yt-dlp/yt-dlp/blob/2026.11.23/yt_dlp/extractor/youtube/_video.py)), through MediaRequests, and appends them to a plain file, going on from the file's length after any stop. #56 plays the files with Media3's FileDataSource, and #57 adds up their sizes.
- DownloadRunner runs the queue in the `downloads` table, one video at a time. It has no Android scheduler in it, so every scheduler runs the same code.
- A tap runs it in a UIDT job, DownloadJobService, on Android 14 and later, and in a WorkManager worker with a dataSync foreground service, DownloadWorker, on Android 10 to 13. Phase 7's overnight job will be a WorkManager worker with charging and unmetered constraints, running the same runner, which is what Google recommends for work the app starts.
- A stop by the user from the Task Manager counts as a pause, as the maintainer chose on November 26, 2026. At the process's start, DownloadsAfterStop reads the app's latest exit record and pauses the queue only when its reason is REASON_USER_REQUESTED, a download was running then, and the app wasn't updated since, because Android gives the same reason for a swipe away from the recent apps and, before Android 14, for an update ([ApplicationExitInfo](https://developer.android.com/reference/android/app/ApplicationExitInfo#REASON_USER_REQUESTED)).
- The app adds androidx.work:work-runtime 2.12.0, released September 23, 2026, under Apache 2.0. Its POM brings androidx.room:room-runtime 2.7.0 for WorkManager's own database, so Room 2 ships inside WorkManager. AGENTS.md's Room 3 rule is about Dewfall's own database, which stays on Room 3.
- WorkManager writes log lines of its own that the app can't turn off: in 2.12.0, `Logger.get()` makes a DEBUG LogcatLogger when none is set, and the setup logs through it before `setMinimumLoggingLevel` applies ([work-runtime 2.12.0 sources](https://dl.google.com/android/maven2/androidx/work/work-runtime/2.12.0/work-runtime-2.12.0-sources.jar)). So the app doesn't set a level, and the work carries nothing about a video: a constant name, no tags and no input data. The hand checks of November 26, 2026 saw its DEBUG lines in Dewfall's process all through a download, on Android 10, 12L and 13, with work IDs and class names only. This sits beside [ADR 0007](0007-how-the-app-logs.md)'s rules, as Media3's logging does.

## Consequences

- Two schedulers start a tap's download, by Android version. Both are thin, and the runner and writer, which hold the logic, are shared and tested on the JVM. The emulator tests run on Android 10 and 17 in CI, so both paths run there.
- On Android 10 to 13, a run that Android starts in the background, such as after a reboot, doesn't get a foreground service on Android 12 and 13 ([background start restrictions](https://developer.android.com/develop/background-work/services/fgs/restrictions-bg-start)), so the worker asks for one only while the app is in the foreground, because WorkManager 2.12 doesn't pass a job's stop to a run whose foreground service Android refused. Expedited work "best fits short tasks", so a long download there may stop every 10 minutes or so and go on in the next run. It loses at most one range each time.
- Without the notification permission, which a new install on Android 13 and later doesn't have and the app declares but never asks for, Android shows a running download only in its Task Manager, while it runs as a UIDT job or in a foreground service. A worker that Android starts in the background on Android 12 or 13 runs as a plain job, which no notification or Task Manager shows. A check on November 26, 2026, on an Android 17 emulator, ran a UIDT job for 7 minutes in the background without the permission, and the Task Manager listed the app with a Stop button. Phase 7's onboarding decides whether to ask.
- A tap's download runs on any connection, charging or not, unlike the overnight downloads that AGENTS.md, the roadmap's decision 7 and ADR 0005 describe. Their dated notes say so.
- The app's APK carries Room 2 inside WorkManager, and the APK grows by WorkManager's size.
- If NewPipeExtractor moves to SABR, YouTube's newer way of streaming, the writer's single-file ranges may need to change with StreamLinks.

## Note, November 28, 2026: backup jobs on Android 14 and later

#269 found that on Android 14, when the app's process ends during a download, the user-initiated job doesn't run again until the screen changes or the app opens, 4 to 7 minutes in #258's hand checks. The downloads spec says it goes on by itself. The cause is in JobScheduler, at commit [6e47c707](https://android.googlesource.com/platform/frameworks/base/+/6e47c7075b91983ae501114425ea25e6df7690c8/apex/jobscheduler/service/java/com/android/server/job/) of Android 14:

- JobServiceContext.onServiceDisconnected closes the job with "unexpectedly disconnected", a system stop.
- JobSchedulerService.getRescheduleJobForFailureLocked computes `backoffAttempts = numFailures + numSystemStops / SYSTEM_STOP_TO_FAILURE_RATIO`, with a ratio of 3, so the first two system stops give no backoff, and the rescheduled job gets `NO_EARLIEST_RUNTIME`: no timing delay, so no alarm wakes it.
- onJobCompletedLocked tracks the rescheduled job and posts no check, so the job stays ready and not pending until some other event makes JobScheduler look at its jobs. Android 13 computed `getNumFailures() + 1`, so WorkManager's job there always had a delay and went on 20 to 45 seconds later. AOSP main at [1cdfff55](https://android.googlesource.com/platform/frameworks/base/+/1cdfff555f4a21f71ccc978290e2e212e2f8b168/apex/jobscheduler/service/java/com/android/server/job/JobSchedulerService.java) has the same code. Android 17's emulator starts the job again at once, for a reason not found.
- The app can't schedule the user-initiated job again from the background: such jobs must be scheduled "while the application is visible to the user" ([UIDT](https://developer.android.com/develop/background-work/background-tasks/uidt)), and they can't have a minimum latency.

So on Android 14 and later, while a run of the user-initiated job downloads, DownloadJobService keeps a plain JobScheduler job, a backup, armed 30 seconds ahead: job 55, scheduled again every 10 seconds, which replaces the waiting one, so while the run lives it never starts. A backup has NETWORK_TYPE_ANY, the queue's backoff and PRIORITY_HIGH, and isn't persisted, because the persisted user-initiated job covers a reboot. When the process ends, the backup starts about 30 seconds later and runs the same DownloadRunner, keeping the other backup, 56, armed the same way, so a process that ends during a backup run goes on too. A backup does nothing while any other run is registered, so the two backups never stop each other. A run that returns or throws disarms the backup it kept, unless another registered run keeps the same one, as a user-initiated run that started during a backup run keeps 55; it checks and disarms on the main thread, where runs register. When the user-initiated job starts again, it sets its notification, arms 55, which stops a running 55, and cancels 56, so it takes the download back; a backup that ends DONE cancels the idle user-initiated job, under the run state's lock. When the app shows, or the user taps a download control, during a backup run, QueueScheduler schedules the user-initiated job, which takes it back. The maintainer chose the notification on November 28, 2026, in the coordinating session's chat: "I meant your recommendation. Go with yours. Tell it to stop for option 1.", and approved the OpenSpec change resume-after-process-end in #269's question dialog: "Approve (Recommended)".

- **The notification.** Any running job may give Android a notification with `setNotification`, not only a user-initiated one, and Android takes it away under JOB_END_NOTIFICATION_POLICY_REMOVE when the job ends or its process dies, unless another running job uses the same ID (JobServiceContext.doSetNotification and JobNotificationCoordinator at 6e47c707). So a backup run gives Android the same notification and ID as the user-initiated job, first at its first progress report, once it claimed a download. The Task Manager doesn't list a backup job. Where Android doesn't mark the notification as a user-initiated job's, the user can swipe it away, so after its first post the run sets it again only while it is among the app's active notifications. Android sheds only updates of a posted notification when an app posts too fast, never a new one (NotificationManagerService.checkDisqualifyingFeatures at 6e47c707, lines 7228 to 7243), so a first post that doesn't show yet is still on its way; in the Android 14 hand check below it kept FLAG_USER_INITIATED_JOB, so a swipe didn't dismiss it.
- **A swipe from the recent apps.** A backup run's process is in the background, because a plain job binds with `BIND_NOT_FOREGROUND | BIND_NOT_PERCEPTIBLE`, so a swipe kills it at once with REASON_USER_REQUESTED, as ActivityManagerService.killProcessesForRemovedTask does for background processes. DownloadsAfterStop would take that for a Stop. So a backup run writes an empty file, `download-backup-run` in noBackupFilesDir, as it begins, after the stop check and under the run lock, unless the user-initiated job's run is registered, which it checks on the main thread, where runs register. A user-initiated run deletes the file as soon as the stop check has read it, without waiting for the run lock that a stopped backup run may still hold, because the Task Manager lists the app from that job's start. At a process's start, the queue doesn't pause when the marker is there, a backup job is still scheduled, and the exit record's importance is IMPORTANCE_PERCEPTIBLE or less important: the Task Manager lists the app only while it runs a user-initiated job or a foreground service ([UIDT](https://developer.android.com/develop/background-work/background-tasks/uidt), [foreground services](https://developer.android.com/develop/background-work/services/fgs/handle-user-stopping)), which make the process more important, and Force stop cancels every job of the app ("app force stopped" in JobSchedulerService), so that exit can only be a swipe. On the Android 14 emulator, a swipe during a backup run recorded importance 230, IMPORTANCE_PERCEPTIBLE, a Stop during a backup run while the player's foreground service ran recorded 125, and a Stop during a run of job 54 recorded 200 ([ApplicationExitInfo.getImportance](https://developer.android.com/reference/android/app/ApplicationExitInfo#getImportance())). The importance check was added after CodeRabbit's review of #271, which found that the player's foreground service let the Task Manager list the app during a backup run, so that Stop didn't pause. Its second review found that a Stop before a run's first claim left no DOWNLOADING row, so it didn't pause either, and backup 55 then went on with the download about 30 seconds later. Main had the same gap, which then waited for the app to open. So the runner now writes an empty run record, `download-run` in noBackupFilesDir, once the stop check has decided and before its first claim, and deletes it when the run ends; the next start's decision counts it as a running download, then deletes it. This fix was checked on the JVM only, in RunRecordTest, because a Stop before the first claim is too short a moment to hit by hand. A Stop in a new process before its decision still finds the rows that the ended process left DOWNLOADING, and the exit's importance, 200 for a user-initiated job, keeps the backup marker from applying. The marker holds nothing about a video ([ADR 0007](0007-how-the-app-logs.md#user-data)). Phase 7's overnight job will run the runner on Android 14 and later too, and must decide what it does with the marker.
- **A tapped download.** A backup job goes on with the download the user tapped, so, like the user-initiated job, it runs on any connection and charging or not, and the rule that downloads the app starts by itself wait for unmetered networks and charging doesn't apply to it. AGENTS.md's note of November 28, 2026 says so. The maintainer accepted it on November 28, 2026, in the coordinating session's question dialog, answering "#269 (PR #271): the backup job continues a download you tapped, so it runs on any connection, charging or not, like the tap itself. Do you accept that?" with "Yes, any connection (Recommended)".
- **Limits.** A plain job runs for up to 30 minutes while the phone charges or the app is in the foreground, and otherwise until its standby bucket's quota runs out, with 10 minutes guaranteed (`RUNTIME_FREE_QUOTA_MAX_LIMIT_MS` and `RUNTIME_MIN_GUARANTEE_MS` in JobSchedulerService, and QuotaController's 10 minutes in each window of 10 minutes for ACTIVE, 2 hours for WORKING_SET, 8 hours for FREQUENT and 24 hours for RARE, at 6e47c707). A long download in a backup job may stop partway and go on from its bytes in the next run, losing at most one range. The backup's timing delay is a non-wakeup alarm, `AlarmManager.ELAPSED_REALTIME` in TimeController.setDelayExpiredAlarmLocked ([TimeController.java](https://android.googlesource.com/platform/frameworks/base/+/6e47c7075b91983ae501114425ea25e6df7690c8/apex/jobscheduler/service/java/com/android/server/job/controllers/TimeController.java), line 362), so a phone whose screen is off may run it only when it wakes. Doze, the RARE and RESTRICTED buckets, and Data Saver or background data turned off on mobile data, because a plain job binds without BIND_BYPASS_USER_NETWORK_RESTRICTIONS, which only a user-initiated job gets ([JobServiceContext.java](https://android.googlesource.com/platform/frameworks/base/+/6e47c7075b91983ae501114425ea25e6df7690c8/apex/jobscheduler/service/java/com/android/server/job/JobServiceContext.java), lines 419 to 445), can hold a backup job much longer. FlexibilityController is off by default, `DEFAULT_FLEXIBILITY_ENABLED = false` on Android 14 with one 72-hour fallback, and `DEFAULT_APPLIED_CONSTRAINTS = 0` on AOSP main, whose fallbacks are 6 hours for PRIORITY_HIGH and 12 hours for PRIORITY_DEFAULT; where a server config turns it on, a backup may wait hours. The keeper calls schedule() about 6 times a minute; Android 14's limit, `DEFAULT_API_QUOTA_SCHEDULE_COUNT = 250` calls per `DEFAULT_API_QUOTA_SCHEDULE_WINDOW_MS` of a minute, applies only to persisted jobs ([JobSchedulerService.java](https://android.googlesource.com/platform/frameworks/base/+/6e47c7075b91983ae501114425ea25e6df7690c8/apex/jobscheduler/service/java/com/android/server/job/JobSchedulerService.java), lines 712 and 713, and scheduleAsPackage's `if (job.isPersisted() ...)` at lines 1557 to 1559), and backup jobs aren't persisted.
- **Gaps.** After a Stop from the Task Manager, backup 55 starts the app's process again about 30 seconds later, finds the queue paused, and does nothing that the user sees. A swipe during a backup run while the player's foreground service runs wasn't checked: ActivityManagerService then sets the process to be killed only once it is in the background (killProcessesForRemovedTask at 6e47c707), so its exit record should show a background importance and not pause, but if Android records a foreground one, the queue pauses.
- **What the hand checks showed**, on November 28, 2026, with real videos:
  - On the Android 14 emulator, main's build reproduced #269: after a kill, job 54 stayed "Ready: true ... !pending=true", "started as UIJ: false", with no start for 5 minutes 20 seconds, until the screen went off and on.
  - With the fix, and the app's notifications not allowed, job 54 stayed stuck the same way. When backup 55 came due, 35 to 47 seconds after the kill, JobScheduler started 54 and 55 in the same moment; 54 took the download back, and 55 stopped or did nothing. Once, 54 stayed stuck and 55 downloaded to the end alone, then cancelled the idle 54. The backup's coming due is what makes JobScheduler look at its jobs again, so most of the time the user-initiated job, with its notification and Task Manager entry, goes on with the download.
  - With the notifications allowed, job 54 started again 15 to 63 milliseconds after each kill, by itself, on Android 14 too.
  - A forced backup run, `adb shell cmd jobscheduler timeout -u 0 io.github.dewfallapp 54` and then `adb shell cmd jobscheduler run -f -u 0 io.github.dewfallapp 55`, showed "Downloading a video" with its progress, and the Task Manager didn't list the app. Android 14 kept FLAG_USER_INITIATED_JOB on the notification, so a swipe didn't dismiss it. `adb shell cmd jobscheduler cancel` stops a job with STOP_REASON_USER, so it pauses the queue instead, as it should.
  - On Android 14 and 17, the Task Manager's Stop left the download paused: backup 55 started the app's process about 45 seconds later, finished in under a second, and added no bytes. On Android 14, a swipe during a backup run ended its process with reason 10, subreason 22, and the download went on without "Paused"; Force stop during a backup run cancelled both backups and paused the queue; opening the app during a backup run gave the download back to job 54, "started as UIJ: true"; a dropped connection during a run of job 54 and during a backup run showed "Waiting to download" and went on when it came back, never "Paused"; a reboot during a freshly scheduled run of job 54 went on at boot without opening the app. After the changes for CodeRabbit's review, a Stop from the Task Manager during a forced backup run, with the player's foreground service running, paused the queue at the next start ("Downloads paused after the app was stopped"), where the build before them went on downloading; a swipe during a forced backup run still went on without "Paused"; and during a run of job 54 that took over from a backup run, the marker was already gone.
  - On Android 17, job 54 started again at once after each kill, as before, and no backup job ran while it lived.
  - On Android 10, 12L and 13, WorkManager's worker went on after a kill, a dropped connection and a reboot, a swipe while a download waited didn't pause it, and the Task Manager's Stop on Android 13 paused it, as before; no backup job, marker or backup notification appeared. A gap that this change doesn't touch showed up on Android 12L: after a reboot the worker runs in the background without a foreground service, so a swipe from the recent apps ends its process with REASON_USER_REQUESTED and the next start pauses the queue. It is [#281](https://github.com/dewfallapp/dewfall/issues/281).
  - A separate Android 14 platform bug showed up: a user-initiated job that JobScheduler rescheduled with a backoff, after a timeout or a run that asked to run again, is persisted with its delay, and at boot JobStore drops it, "A user-initiated job cannot have a time delay". A reboot then leaves the download waiting until the app opens. Main has it too. It is [#280](https://github.com/dewfallapp/dewfall/issues/280).

# Privacy

Dewfall collects no data about you.

- There are no accounts. You never sign in.
- There are no analytics, no ads and no tracking.
- Everything the app stores stays on your device. This includes your channels, watch history, downloaded videos and saved subtitles.
- Android's backups to Google Drive and transfers to a new phone leave all of it out.
- A video you download is kept in the app's own storage, where no other app can read it, with its title, channel, length, publish time and thumbnail links in the app's database. Its folder's name is random, not the video's ID.
- If you export your data, it goes to a file you pick. The app sends it nowhere else.
- If you import your channels from a Google Takeout file, the app reads the file you pick once, on your device, and keeps only the channels' IDs and names. It keeps no copy of the file and sends it nowhere.

## The media notification

While a video plays or is paused, until its notification goes away, Android shows the video's title and channel name in the media notification and on the lock screen, even when you hide sensitive notification content. Android also shares them with Bluetooth devices you connect, such as a car or a watch, and with apps that may read your notifications or control media, such as apps you gave notification access and some of the phone's own apps. The video's ID and links stay in the app.

## The download notification

While a video downloads, Android shows a notification that says "Downloading a video" with its progress. It never names the video. On Android 13 and later, Dewfall doesn't ask for permission to show notifications, so until you allow them in the system settings, Android shows the download only in its list of active apps. On Android 14 and later, a download that goes on by itself after the app's process ended may run where Android shows it nowhere, neither in the notifications nor in the list of active apps, until you open the app or, if you allow notifications by then, until the download's next run. Like any download you started, it may use any connection, mobile data included.

## Network connections

Dewfall connects directly to YouTube to search, fetch channel and video details, download videos, stream them, and fetch subtitles. These requests go from your phone to YouTube with no Dewfall server in between. YouTube can see them, as it can for any app or browser that loads YouTube videos.

To show video thumbnails and channel pictures, Dewfall also loads images straight from YouTube's image servers, i.ytimg.com, yt3.ggpht.com and yt3.googleusercontent.com, and from no other server. It loads only the image links that YouTube's answers gave. These requests carry no cookies and no account. Like any request, they show YouTube your IP address and which images you load, so YouTube can see which videos and channels appear in your lists. Dewfall loads images on any connection, mobile data included.

Loaded images are kept in Dewfall's cache on your device, up to 64 MiB, so a list you open again doesn't load them again. Other apps can't read that cache, Android may clear it at any time, and backups leave it out.
